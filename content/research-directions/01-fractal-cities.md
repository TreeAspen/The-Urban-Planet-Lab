description: "Within a single city, some neighborhoods run several degrees hotter than others just a few blocks away. In this line of work we showed that these intra-urban hotspots—'heat islets'—are statistically self-similar across diverse global cities, following fractal geometry and consistent scaling laws. The framework gives a common language for comparing very different cities and reveals how urban form can lower average temperatures while amplifying local extremes."
